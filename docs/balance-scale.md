# Dignity of Risk Balance Scale

Built from the PRD *Dignity of Risk vs. Duty of Care Balance Scale* (20 November 2026). This note records how v1 works, the decisions made, and what is left. It covers the PRD's build steps 1 to 4: the Mrs Alvarez case, the scale and its text equivalent, the criterion check, what to say, the new fact, the debrief, the two contrasting cases, and the sign-off gate.

In the Magazine editor: **Add a block → Simulate → Dignity of Risk Balance Scale**.

## What it is

A Magazine block where a learner works out how to support a person's choice that carries risk. A good plan protects what matters to the person and responds to the risk in proportion. Dignity of risk isn't a reason to do nothing, and duty of care isn't a reason to control someone's life.

It's a training case with made-up people. Nothing in it changes a real care plan, incident register, supplier record or clinical task. Finishing it isn't a clinical competency sign-off. The block says both.

## The four steps

1. **Understand.** The situation in the person's own words, the current plan and the risk signal with its urgency. The learner asks at least one question ("What is it about the walker, outside?") and reads what it turns up before planning anything.
2. **Name the tension.** One goal card (whose goal is it?) and one or more risk cards, grouped as risk to the person, risk to staff or others during funded care, and conditions on funded services. Choosing a risk the facts don't support (for example "she may not be able to decide") or missing one they do sends the learner back. They're told why for what they chose, and how many risks they missed, but not which.
3. **Build the plan.** Cards in four groups: Listen and inform, Practical adjustments, Clinical and provider action, Record and review. Add and remove in any order. There's no set number of cards. When a card needs the person's answer, it shows what they say. **If they decline, the card doesn't count**: an OT review offered and declined is recorded as "offered, declined", not as done.
4. **Test the plan.** The learner chooses what they'd say to the person, sees feedback on it, then a **new fact** arrives (Mrs Alvarez has a fall). New cards appear, marked "New". The plan isn't balanced again until the must-do steps for the new fact are in it, and the case can't be finished without them.

A plan with something unsafe in it (a waiver and nothing else, "leave it alone", sending a worker into a known hazard) can't be taken to the test step or finished. The learner is told why.

## The scale

Two pans: **What matters to the person** and **How we support safety**. The beam tilts as the plan changes. It doesn't move at all for anyone who has asked for reduced motion.

The beam is a helper, not the assessment. Next to it, in words:
- **The state**: "Balanced plan for this scenario", "Leaning towards control", "Leaning towards leaving the risk unmanaged", "Both sides need more", or which side needs more.
- **A sentence for each side.**
- **Up to two questions to think about**, taken from the criteria that aren't met yet. They never name a card or give a count.

When the plan is balanced, it always adds: "Residual risk remains. These steps explain the choice, support agreed safeguards and set out when to review or escalate." I used "Balanced plan for this scenario", as the PRD suggested, rather than "Safe & Audit-Ready".

"Your plan" lists the same plan as plain text, including which offers were agreed and which were declined.

## The criteria behind it

The case's author writes criteria, not weights. Each criterion:
- sits on one side of the scale;
- is met by any one of a set of cards, so more than one sensible plan works;
- has the question the learner sees when it's missing.

Some criteria apply only after the new fact. They're marked must-do, and nothing else stands in for them.

**A plan is balanced only when every active criterion is met and nothing over-restrictive or unsafe is in it.** No stack of minor cards makes up for a missing must-do step: in Mrs Alvarez's case, adding "another path change" after the fall makes the plan less balanced, not more.

The criteria, what each card is worth, the person's answers and the new fact stay on the server. The learner's page gets the wording of the situation and the cards. The scale comes back from the server each time the plan changes.

## The debrief

- **The final state of the scale**, including when it isn't balanced. A learner can finish an unbalanced plan once there's nothing unsafe in it and the must-do steps are there. The debrief shows exactly what's missing.
- **Five areas, each on its own**: Choice support, Risk recognition, Proportionate mitigation, Documentation and review, Escalation and provider action. Each is "Strong", "Partly there" or "Worth another look", with notes. Notes cover a missing criterion, a card that was in the plan at some point and shouldn't have been, a first phrase that was controlling or dismissive, how many tries the tension took, and whether any question got to the person's reasons.
- **Patterns** of leaning towards control and of leaving risk unmanaged, if either happened.
- **Every criterion**, marked met or missing.
- **Their plan**, with why each card helps or doesn't, and what the person said to anything they were asked. Also the cards they left out, with why.
- **The questions** and why each did or didn't help, the person's goal, what they said (first and final), and the example phrase.
- **What remains**: residual risk, the review owner and the review trigger.
- **A care-plan note, labelled as a fictional teaching example**, with a line saying it isn't a real record and nothing is added to one.
- **Try a different plan** starts again. A finished attempt is kept.

There's no number. A weighted result isn't stored either: what's kept is each area's rating, whether the plan was balanced, and the patterns.

## Sign-off, and the check that can't be waived

Every case has a **Source and sign-off** section:
- the policy source, its version, an internal policy id, and effective and review dates;
- the content owner, and the Clinical, Quality and Care Operations reviewers, and the date;
- **a WHS reviewer, required only when the case includes a real risk to workers or others** (Mr Okoro and Mrs Pearce, not Mrs Alvarez).

**A course with a Balance Scale can't be published until those are recorded.** As with Spot the Signal and the Mission Board, "Publish anyway" doesn't get past this.

Publishing is also blocked (the ordinary way) if:
- there's no question worth asking;
- there isn't exactly one right goal card;
- no risk is marked as one the learner must name;
- there's no unsafe card before the new fact;
- a criterion has no card, or could be met by an over-restrictive or unsafe card;
- the criteria aren't on both sides of the scale;
- there's no good phrase;
- a new fact has no must-do criterion;
- the review owner, review trigger or teaching note is missing.

## The three starter cases

All three are **fictional drafts with no sign-off recorded**.

| Case | What it tests |
|---|---|
| **Mrs Alvarez: the garden** | The PRD's first scenario: a routine, informed preference with an emerging falls risk. The OT review is offered and she declines it. There's more than one valid plan (path changes, or sturdier shoes, as the agreed mitigation). Then a fall with a sore wrist: post-fall assessment, an incident record and a plan review become must-do. |
| **Mr Okoro: the neighbour** | The PRD's unvetted-contractor template. He can prefer his neighbour, and his neighbour can keep helping as a friend, but a funded booking can't happen until the checks are met. New fact: "He's applied, can he start Monday?" Applied isn't cleared. |
| **Mrs Pearce: the hallway** | The PRD's cluttered-home template. Her home and belongings, kept separate from her worker's safe way out. Clearing her things or stopping all services is over-restrictive; "carry on and be careful" is unsafe. New fact: the hallway is blocked again and she wants a quick shower. |

**The facts, cards, criteria and notes are mine, drawn from the PRD, and need checking by Clinical, Quality and Care Operations (and WHS for the last two) before anyone signs them off.** In particular:
- the post-fall steps should match the approved clinical protocol;
- the funded-worker requirements should match what Supplier Compliance actually needs;
- the two-week check-ins are the cases' own, not a policy.

## For authors

A case has:
- **The situation:** a title and introduction, the person and their own words, the situation, the current plan, the risk signal and its urgency, other facts, and who the learner is.
- **What the learner works with:** the questions (what each turns up, and whether it's worth asking), the goal and risk cards, and the plan cards (group, what each is in this case, what it shows, why it helps or doesn't, whether the person is asked and what they say, and whether it's offered only after the new fact).
- **How it's checked:** the criteria (side, what it shows, the question when it's missing, the cards that meet it, whether it applies after the new fact and whether it's must-do).
- **Testing and debrief:** the phrases, the new fact, the debrief (residual risk, review owner, trigger, the teaching note), and source and sign-off.

People in cases are always made up.

## What isn't built

- **AI-drafted cases.** Every word is authored. The PRD's authoring assistant isn't built.
- **A screen for the author totals.** The totals are available at `/api/magazine/<course>/balance?stats=1`, for learning design review: how many finished, finished balanced, leaned towards control, left risk unmanaged, or needed more than one try at the tension. There are no names and no words.
- **Flags on cases when a policy changes,** and an approval workflow with stages. Sign-off is recorded names and a date, as with the other blocks.
- **Linking to a Self-Managed vs Clinical Governance Line-Drawer.** That block doesn't exist in the Hub.
- **Audience-specific versions** (external coordinators, care workers). Authors can write a separate case for each.
- **The accessibility checker and a text-only preview in the editor.** The editor has the usual Preview.

## What was checked, and what wasn't

Checked in the browser as Jordan on a throwaway course (now archived, with its attempts removed):
- question answers coming from the server;
- a made-up capacity risk refused, with why, and a missing risk counted;
- the waiver alone reading "Leaning towards leaving the risk unmanaged";
- the declined OT offer shown as declined and not counting;
- a balanced plan with the residual-risk note;
- a controlling phrase's feedback;
- the fall unbalancing the plan with its must-do questions;
- finishing refused until the post-fall steps were in, and "another path change" flagged as unsafe;
- the debrief showing an honestly unbalanced plan (I'd taken the review card out);
- the scale leaning towards control;
- no sideways scrolling at phone width;
- the editor's sections, starter cases and palette entry.

**Found and fixed during the check:** several quick clicks in a row only kept the last one, because each started from the same snapshot of the plan. Changes now build on the latest state. A partial save also wiped fields it didn't mention; that's fixed and covered by the tests.

Not checked in the browser: Mr Okoro's and Mrs Pearce's cases (covered by tests), the editor's preview mode, editing a case field by field, and a real screen reader (structure only: labelled sections, live regions for the scale and the person's answers, pressed states on card buttons).
